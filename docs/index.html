Built around how Georgia eats

Star ratings won't tell you which wing spot Atlanta argues over or which Buford Highway plaza hides the noodles. Georgia Eats is organized the way people here eat: soul food and meat-and-threes, Buford Highway, lemon pepper wings and Lowcountry seafood, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Every place links to the health department's own inspection reports, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out in the mountains or on the marsh.

What stays on your phone

Georgia Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Georgia Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The soul food, Buford Highway, lemon pepper wing, Lowcountry, barbecue and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: its website or menu, a dated local news story or an official listing. The wider directory of 23,274 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against Fulton County's food permits and the state's alcohol licenses, and adds licensed restaurants and bars the map data missed.

Where can I get lemon pepper wings?

Our lemon pepper list has 21 hand-checked spots in 11 towns around Atlanta, from J.R. Crickets to Campbellton Road, every one a finalist in the Atlanta Journal-Constitution's 2026 taste test or a lemon pepper pick in its 2026 wing guide. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a Georgia restaurant's inspection?

County health departments give each inspection a 0 to 100 score and an official letter grade (A, B, C or U), and the latest report has to hang within fifteen feet of the front door. The reports are in the Department of Public Health's search (and GNR Public Health's, for Gwinnett, Newton and Rockdale). They aren't published as data, so we link them from every place rather than copy them, and we compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

When the state says so. The Georgia Department of Revenue publishes every active state alcohol license each quarter, so a place with a license to serve on the premises says so. Cities and counties license too, and the state's list includes licenses still pending, so a place with no license matched shows as unknown, never as no.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
